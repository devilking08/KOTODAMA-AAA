# Story — KOTODAMA: Echoes of the Nameless

> The Hush does not kill. It forgets. It is far worse.

---

## Premise

A world is being erased — not destroyed, but *forgotten*. Names fade from
graves, roads forget where they lead, mothers forget the shape of their
children's hands. The force behind it is called **the Hush**: not a monster,
not a god, but the sound a story makes when the last person who could tell it
stops listening.

You are **Kuro**, a swordsman who wakes on a beach with a sword that has no
name, a bell that will not stop ringing, and a fox who knows more than she
says. Your name was taken by the Hush before you woke. That is the only
reason you are still here: a name is a handle, and the Hush cannot grasp what
has no handle — it can only ignore it.

The game's question, asked by every region and every boss, is the same one:
**What do you do with a thing that has been forgotten?**

---

## The world

### The six regions

1. **The Shore of Broken Words** — a fishing coast half-drowned by an inland
   sea. A village whose dead cannot be buried because the sea keeps them. A
   lantern that has been out since the road sank.
2. **The Drowned Bell** — a black island in a black sea. Its temple bell
   rings every thirteen minutes; each toll is a name being erased. Kane, the
   Bellkeeper, has kept it ringing for a century because he is terrified of
   what silence means.
3. **The White Silence** — a mountain pass where it has not rained, sung, or
   spoken in thirty years. A bride waits at a Frozen Chapel for a groom who
   took the wrong road. The snow keeps her bouquet fresh, and keeps everyone
   else too.
4. **The Drowned Dunes** — an ocean of sand over a buried kingdom. A queen
   sold her oasis to a god for one rain; the god forgot to pay. The dunes
   are her interest. In the middle of them, a garden that should not exist.
5. **The Bamboo Requiem** — green rain, green light, green silence. A
   forest that learned to keep time; it taps the names of the forgotten in a
   rhythm the monks call the Requiem. Two twins share one name and cannot
   agree who keeps it. A shogun wears a new face every year because no one
   can ask him *why* if they never see the same face twice.
6. **The Hollow Crown** — a volcano palace built around a name that was
   never spoken. Lava falls in slow curtains. Every hall is a different
   memory of the same wedding — the wedding that was supposed to end the
   war and instead ended all the guests.

### The Hush

The Hush is never depicted as a villain. It has no face. It is a natural
disaster of meaning. Its tool is the **bell**: every toll is a syllable of
some name falling out of the world. The protagonists' fight is not to kill
it (you cannot) but to decide what the world is *for* once it has been
quiet.

## Characters

| Character | Role | Arc |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Kuro** | You. A swordsman with no name. | Recovering your name is the true ending. The game is framed as a question you answer by playing: who are you *when no one is watching*? |
| **Yomi** | A fox who is an archivist of the Hush. Your guide, comic relief, and conscience. | She claims to be neutral. She is not. Her final line in the true ending is the thesis: "You were not a lock. You were a door." |
| **Ame** | Shrine keeper at the Shore, 16. The heart of the story. | Her mother's name was taken by the Hush. Ame keeps the lantern for everyone the sea took. Her side quest (`sq_ame_name`) is the *only* path to your own name. |
| **Ushio** | Ch.1 boss. A general who drowned holding a beach against memories. | Posture-break him and the tide gives back his name. His arc teaches the game's thesis: names are retained by being *said*. |
| **Kane** | Ch.2 boss. The Bellkeeper. | He rings the bell because stopping feels like giving up on the people whose names it has already taken. |
| **Yuki** | Ch.3 boss. The bride. | She waits because the wait is the only thing she has left of the wedding. |
| **Kaji** | Ch.4 boss. The Ember Monk. | He burned his monastery to keep one child warm. The fire never went out because it is fed by the memory of the choice. |
| **Sabaku** | Ch.5 boss. Queen of the dunes. | Her contract with the god is the world's clearest example of how the Hush does business: it fulfills *nothing*, and compounds the debt. |
| **Mori** | Ch.6 boss. The Last Gardener. | She has kept the last water alive for a century. Nobody is wrong: the caravan needs the water, and the garden is the only proof that rain ever existed. |
| **Futari** | Ch.7 boss. Twin blades, one name. | They have spent thirty years deciding who keeps the name. The forest repeats their rhythm. Splitting them is the kindness: they both get to be the name, together. |
| **Kage** | Ch.8 boss, then ally in Ch.10. The Masked Shogun. | He wears your face this year. His question (side quest `sq_kage_answer`) is the final mirror: what would you do with a king's power? |
| **Yū** | Ch.9 boss. Bride of Ash. | She waited at the altar for the wedding that ended everyone. Defeating her is not punishing her; it is letting the wedding be over. |
| **The Hollow Crown** | Ch.10 boss. That Which Forgot Itself. | The throne with no king. The crown with no head. It has been waiting for a name — yours will do. It is the Hush's last, tired attempt to be a *thing* instead of an absence. |

## The four endings

1. **Speak — "The World, Spoken."** You speak the world's name. The Hush
   becomes the background noise of a living world, not the world's only
   listener. Everyone gets their names back, imperfectly, the way water
   returns to a dry riverbed: in a rush, then slowly, then as a life.
2. **Silence — "The Quiet End."** You let it finish. The world ends
   gently. The last word is not sad — it is *finished*. This is the ending
   for the player who decided the world deserved to stop being a sentence.
3. **Become — "The Keeper."** You take the crown and hold the silence
   yourself, so that someday someone else is ready. The mountain closes its
   mouth around you. This is the ending for the player who chose duty.
4. **Remember — "My Name Is Kuro."** You speak your own name. The Hush
   recoils because it was looking for the name at the center of everything
   and never thought it would be walking around in a chipped sword, being
   ordinary on purpose. The world becomes *speakable*. This is the true
   ending, and it is the only one that requires paying attention — not
   skill.

---

## Themes

- **Names as commitment.** To name something is to agree to keep it. The
  game literalizes this: memory exists only when it is spoken.
- **The difference between forgetting and being forgotten.** Characters who
  lose names (Ame's mother, Tobi's arm, Kane's count) are not victimized;
  they are given the harder task: living with the shape of the absence.
- **Silence as a choice, not a failure.** Three of four endings are quiet.
  The game refuses to rank them morally; it asks which one *you* can live
  with.
- **Ordinariness as heroism.** Kuro's true power is not the sword. It is
  being a person with a name, in a world that stopped having any.

---

## Tone & language

The dialogue is spare, wry, and occasionally absurd (Yomi). No character
exposits the lore; the world is overheard. Boss barks are short sentences,
not speeches. The codex fills the gaps for those who want them — but the
story is complete without it.
